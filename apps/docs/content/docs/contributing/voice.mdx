Zach asked for the docs to be written the way Richard Feynman explained things: sensitive, graceful, and occasionally funny. This page is that request, turned into rules a person (or an agent) can follow. It is about the words. It is not permission to change a fact.

The voice is for introductions, overviews, getting-started pages, concept pages, and the sentences that carry a reader from one step to the next. A reference table, an error code, an API page, and a CLI flag stay precise and compact. A joke in a parameter table helps nobody.

## What the voice actually is

Explain from first principles, as if to a smart friend who has not seen the system. Lead with why a thing exists and what it does. The how comes after the reader knows what they are about to do, and why they would bother.

**Sensitivity.** Respect the reader. Never condescend. Do not write "simply" or "just" in the way that makes a hard thing sound like the reader's fault. When something is genuinely tricky — tenancy, key types, two different objects called a project — say so. People learn faster when the difficulty is named.

**Grace.** Clear, warm, unhurried sentences. Cut jargon when a plain word exists, and cut marketing language always. "Blazingly fast," "seamless," "next-generation," "unlock," and "elevate" do not teach. A concrete example does. Prefer "a different org is a different database file" to "robust tenant isolation."

**Humor.** Light, honest, occasional. A wry aside or a playful analogy is welcome when it makes the idea stick and then gets out of the way. One per page is usually enough. If the joke has to be explained, it was not a joke, it was a delay. Do not impersonate Feynman, and do not invent quotations from him or from anyone else.

**Honesty.** Say what we know, what we measured, and what we have not. A number from the gateway, a timing from a specific prompt, and a worked example are different kinds of fact — label them. If two numbers on a page measure different things, say what each one measures. Never write a limitation you have not checked. The traps in [Style](/docs/contributing/style) still apply; a friendly sentence that is wrong is worse than a stiff one that is right.

## A few sentences, before and after

Stiff, and a little above the reader:

> Simply point your existing client at our unified, next-generation endpoint to unlock 400+ models instantly.

In this voice:

> The request shape is the ordinary chat-completions one, so a client you already have usually works once its base URL is `https://api.hanzo.ai/v1` and it sends a Hanzo key. There are 500+ models behind that URL. The surprising part is not the count. It is that the call looks the same whichever model you name.

The second version is longer. That is fine. Short is not the same thing as clear, and a reader who understands the first time does not need the paragraph again.

## Facts that the voice must not smudge

Rewriting a sentence is not a chance to upgrade a claim.

- **Enso** is a small language model that also routes. Tiers, per million tokens: `enso-free` $0, `enso` and `enso-flash` $0.33 / $1.65, `enso-pro` $4.40 / $22, `enso-ultra` $5.50 / $27.50. Hanzo's own models, including Enso, carry only a payment processing fee: 3.5% by credit card, or 3% in crypto. There is no routing fee on them. Third-party models follow OpenRouter: 5.5% by credit card, or 5% in crypto, on the standard plan; Business is 8% on credit purchases; enterprise is unpublished, so say contact sales and do not invent a rate. Do not write a 1% routing fee, and do not call Enso a frontier model.
- **The catalogue** is 500+ models. Do not write "over 400," and do not write a "400M+ data points" claim. That claim is not one we make.
- **Code, endpoints, parameters, CLI commands, config keys, URLs, frontmatter keys, slugs, and navigation** stay as they are. So do MDX components and imports. If a procedure is wrong, that is a bug to fix on purpose, not a side effect of a nicer paragraph.
- **Live numbers win.** Prices, model ids, and context windows come from the gateway. A page that disagrees with `/v1/models` is the page that is wrong. Say that, when you cite a rate, so the next editor knows which source to trust.

## What to leave alone

Generated pages — the OpenAPI reference, the MCP catalogue, the CLI pages, the pricing page — are produced from the contract at build time. Edit the generator, or the source it reads, if a sentence there is wrong. Editing the output by hand will not survive the next build.

`projects/` is synced from other repositories. Those pages have their own authors. Lux, Zoo, and Zen docs live in their own apps and brands. This voice is for Hanzo's pages on docs.hanzo.ai.

## A small test

Read the paragraph aloud. If you run out of breath, the sentence is doing too much. If you would be embarrassed to say it to a colleague who is stuck, it is either too cute or too proud. If a newcomer could follow it and an expert would not feel talked down to, it is ready.
